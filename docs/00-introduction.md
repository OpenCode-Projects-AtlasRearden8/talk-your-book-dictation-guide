# Talk Your Book: A Voice Dictation Manual for Authors

*By Asdale Dynamics, LLC — beginner-friendly AI education from Dylan Asdale, author of The Massive AI Manual.*

## Who this manual is for

You have a book (or blog, or guide) inside you, and typing is the slow part. Maybe your hands get tired. Maybe your ideas move faster than your fingers. Maybe you have ADHD, chronic pain, or a shift-work schedule from a nursing career, and the chair-and-keyboard routine drains you before the writing even starts.

This manual is about **talking your book** — speaking drafts out loud and letting free tools turn them into text — then sculpting that raw dictation into readable prose.

Three promises up front:

1. **Everything here is free or built into the device you already own.** No paid subscriptions are required to follow any chapter.
2. **Everything can stay private.** You can choose a fully local setup where your voice never leaves your computer. We show both cloud and local paths and tell you which is which.
3. **No condescension.** We explain things like a friend would: plain English, step by step.

## The four phases of talking your book

Dictation for authors has four phases. Know them, and everything in this manual slots into place:

1. **Speak it.** Generate the raw draft with your voice — the fastest writing many people ever do. Chapter 1 and Chapter 2.
2. **Sculpt it.** Turn rough dictation into clean prose with rules and AI editors. Chapter 3 and Chapter 4.
3. **Protect it.** Keep your manuscript (and your privacy) safe while you work. Chapter 5.
4. **Repeat it.** Build the habit with the 30-day word-flow trial. Chapter 6.

## Honest expectations

Dictation will *not* feel literary on day one. When you speak, you write in longer, simpler sentences than you type — that is a feature, not a bug. Your first drafts will sound like you at your friendliest, which is exactly what most readers want. The sculpting pass (Chapter 4) is where the polish happens. The habit, not the tool, is the real unlock: the authors who win with voice are the ones who did it thirty days in a row.

## How to use this manual

- Read Chapter 1 first if you want to start *today* with zero installs.
- Read Chapter 2 first if you already know you want your voice files kept entirely on your machine.
- Skim the table above, pick your path, and only come back for chapters you need. This is a manual, not a treadmill.

One more thing before you dive in: **draft out loud, edit in silence.** When you speak, be a river. When you edit, be a mason. Trying to write perfectly with your voice is the single most common way people quit. You're not going to quit.
