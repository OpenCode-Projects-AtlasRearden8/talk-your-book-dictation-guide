One month, ~15–30 minutes a day, and dictation stops being a novelty and becomes a writing organ. This is the plan. It's built the way Dylan builds every habit: small, countable, and kind to low-energy days. Skip a day? Zero guilt — pick the plan back up where the day counter says. The plan's worst enemy is perfectionism, not missed days.

6.1 The rules of the trial

1. Dictate every (intended) session. Fuzzy on the calendar — "20 of the next 30 days" is fine. 2. Count words, not quality. Raw dictated words only. Edit nothing mid-trial except the Chapter 4 checklist twice a week. 3. One project the whole month. Your book, a guide, a blog series — pick it on Day 1 and don't re-pick. Switching projects is avoidance wearing a costume. 4. Log every day in the tracker (6.5). One line, 10 seconds.

6.2 Week-by-week plan

Week 1 — Calibration (the voice opens its account)

DaySession (15–20 min)
1Chapter 1 tool on your everyday device. Dictate your project's backstory: why this book, who it's for, what it should change in the reader. Target: 300 raw words.
2Do it again, new material. Practice pause discipline (Ch. 3.2). Target: 400 raw words.
3Dictate on the other device you own (phone vs. computer). Feel both; favorite gets named. Target: 400 words.
4Dictate a rough outline of your first 3 chapters by voice. Lower accuracy tolerance; this is scaffolding. Target: 400 words.
5Free-style: pick the chapter that excites you most and talk it. Target: 500 words.
6Do your first sculpting session per Ch. 4 (both passes) on ONE day's draft. Re-read aloud once.
7Log, rest, no dictation. Patch anything broken (mic, app, folder structure).

Week 2 — Volume (find your sustainable pace)

DayFocus
8–11Daily 600-word sessions on consecutive chapters. Try one "walk-and-talk" day and one "standing desk or kitchen" day; log your word throughput for each in the tracker's notes column.
12First long sculpting block: polish one full dictated chapter (Ch. 4.7 checklist).
13Catch-up day or rest day, at your discretion.
14Log. Celebrate: you now have more finished manuscript than most writers' entire year at the typing pace.

Week 3 — Environment stress tests (voice under life conditions)

  • Day 15: noisy environment (café, kitchen, car with engine on). Expect accuracy dips; Chapters 2.5/2.7 upgrades might be worth it now.
  • Day 16: tired/low-energy day. Practice the shortest possible session: outline only. Track it as a successful day — the plan is ADHD-Friendly by design: a bad-energy day that still produces 100 words is a win, not a failure.
  • Day 17: practice "quote… unquote" dialogue for one scene.
  • Day 18: full sculpting pass; run the Chapter 4.6 reading-aloud loop on the result.
  • Day 19: if you set up a local transcription server (Ch. 2), do a whole memo-to-manuscript cycle including an Ollama sculpture pass. If not, repeat your best phone workflow.
  • Day 20: long session, warm-up rough material you later decide to keep anyway.
  • Day 21: rest, log, re-read week 3's sculpted pages with two-week-old eyes.

Week 4 — Integration (make it your writing life)

  • Days 22–27: a session every day at your favorite time slot, locked to the best workflow that survived this month. Two sculpting sessions per the Ch. 4.7 checklist rhythm.
  • Day 28: do the friend test on the whole trial's output: what feels like a book now, what feels like a brain dump.
  • Day 29: plan Book #/Next Project in 300 dictated words — using the same method, so integration is automatic.
  • Day 30: final tracker line, then read your weekly totals in the tracker. This number is your real writing speed. Post-trial, plan book length around that figure, definitely not the typing-speed figure your wrists believed.

6.3 The tracker (copy this into a text file)

Keep it plain. One line per day. No apps needed.


DATE    DAY#  RAW WORDS  SCULPTED?  NOTES
11-01    01      412      no        too many "so basically"es
11-02    02      398      no        walk-and-talk, at the park
11-03    03      505      no        phone mic decent in the car
...

Weekend tally rule: sum the days. If the sum went UP from the previous week, you're doing it right.

6.4 Common failure modes & fixes

SymptomFix
"I hated listening to my transcripts"Normal until week 2. Everyone hears filler at first. The filler shrinks with practice, the sculpting pass does the rest.
Missed 3+ days in a rowRestart at Week 1, Day 5 (the free-style day) and only count sessions after that. Restarting the plan costs nothing.
Device dying mid-sessionPhones with old batteries die first. Charge while you work, or dictate into the notes app that was already open.
Accuracy rage at namesChapter 3.3's placeholders + one find-replace. Never dictate a name more than twice — dictate it wrong and fix it, faster every time.
Words counting feels vainGood. Count for 30 days, then stop. The number is scaffolding, not identity.

6.5 After the trial

  • Keep the 20-minutes-a-day session, whatever slot it lives in.
  • Keep reading-aloud editing at least every other sculpting pass.
  • Re-run the trial before your next book; each trial is cheaper than the one before.
  • Your manuscript — and eventually your KDP listings and newsletters — all start flowing from this habit. The author's job is to keep showing up with the voice, the sculpting pass, and the calendar. This manual handed you all three.
